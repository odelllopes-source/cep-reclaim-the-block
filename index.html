Community Engagement Project — University of Mumbai, BSc IT

Cleaner public spaces start with the people who use them.

A youth-led volunteering initiative to improve civic hygiene and reclaim shared public spaces in our local community — undertaken as part of the CEP component under NEP 2020.

About the project

Why civic hygiene and public spaces became our focus area.

Litter-strewn parks, broken benches, encroached footpaths and unmanaged waste are everyday sights in most Indian neighbourhoods — not because people don't care, but because no one has taken ownership. This project set out to test a simple idea: that a small group of student volunteers, working directly with residents, can shift that sense of ownership back to the community.

Over the course of the semester, our team studied a chosen public space in the local area, engaged with residents and shopkeepers around it, and ran a series of youth-led volunteering drives to clean, maintain and improve the upkeep of that space — while documenting the process for the CEP report.

What we set out to do

Objectives guiding the field work, aligned with the CEP course outcomes.

Assess the space

Survey a public space to understand hygiene gaps, usage patterns, and resident concerns first-hand.

Mobilise volunteers

Organise fellow students and local youth into a volunteering group for sustained, not one-off, action.

Build local buy-in

Work with residents and shopkeepers so the upkeep continues after the project ends.

Run cleanup drives

Execute hands-on cleanup, waste segregation and basic repair/beautification activity.

Spread awareness

Use posters, short talks and door-to-door conversations to nudge everyday civic habits.

Document and reflect

Record observations, photos and feedback to feed into the CEP report and evaluation.

How the project unfolded

Five stages, carried out across the semester with the faculty mentor's guidance.

Site visit & need assessment

Walked the chosen public space, spoke to regular users, and logged specific hygiene and maintenance issues.

Planning with the faculty mentor

Shared findings with the mentor, finalised the scope of the drives, and set a schedule of visits.

Awareness campaign

Put up posters and spoke with residents and shopkeepers about waste segregation and upkeep.

Cleanup & volunteering drives

Ran hands-on sessions — litter clearance, waste segregation, basic repairs — with student volunteers.

Follow-up & reporting

Returned to check whether the space stayed clean, gathered resident feedback, and compiled the CEP report.
